# How to turn one podcast episode into a week of posts

## Start with the moments, not the minutes

Most creators open the timeline and start cutting from minute one. That is the slow way. Before you touch the editor, read the transcript once and mark the moments where someone changes their mind, tells a story, or gives a clear instruction. Those three kinds of moments travel well on their own. Everything else is context that only works inside the full episode.

A good moment makes sense to a stranger who has never heard your show. Test it by reading the marked lines out loud with no setup. If you need to explain what came before, the moment is not ready. Trim the setup or pick another one.

## Give every moment one job

Each clip or post should do one thing. It can teach a single step, show a result, or prove a point with a short story. When a clip tries to do two jobs, viewers leave before the second one starts. Write the job down in one sentence before you cut anything.

This one sentence becomes your hook. If you cannot write it, the moment is not strong enough yet. Go back to the transcript and look for a sharper line from the same conversation.

## Match the moment to the platform

The same idea needs a different shape on each platform. A story with a turn in the middle works as a vertical video on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. A list of steps works better as a carousel that people save for later. A strong opinion works as a LinkedIn text post where people can reply and argue.

Do not post the same file everywhere. Change the first line, the length and the format for each platform. People who follow you in two places will notice copies and scroll past both.

## Write the first line last

The first line decides whether anyone reads the second. Draft the whole post first, then write five first lines and pick the most specific one. A specific first line names a person, a mistake, or a result. A vague first line asks a question nobody was asking.

For video, the first two seconds do the same job. Put the hook on screen as text, because many people watch with the sound off. Keep it short enough to read in one glance.

## Captions are part of the edit

Burned-in captions are not decoration. They carry the message for every viewer who has the sound muted. Keep each caption to a few words, place it away from the buttons at the bottom of the screen, and make it high contrast.

Check every caption against the audio before you publish. Names, numbers and product terms are where automatic captions go wrong. One wrong word in a caption can make a careful creator look careless.

## Plan the week before you publish anything

Lay out the whole week before the first post goes live. Spread the strongest moments across different days so the week does not peak on Monday. Mix formats so your feed does not look like the same post seven times.

Give every post a link with tracking tags and a goal you can measure. A post without a goal is hard to judge later. A post with a goal tells you what to repeat next week.

## Batch the busywork into one sitting

Uploading, scheduling and writing descriptions eat more time than cutting. Do all of it in one sitting instead of a little every day. Open every platform once, schedule the whole week, and close the tabs.

Keep a short checklist for that sitting so nothing gets missed. Check the captions, the links, the cover image and the posting time for each post. A checklist turns a tiring chore into a routine you can hand to an editor.

## Review the results and keep what works

At the end of the week, look at which hooks held attention and which formats people saved or shared. Keep a short list of the patterns that worked. Use that list when you pick moments from the next episode.

Repurposing is not about doing more work. It is about getting more reach from work you have already done. One episode, cut with care, can carry a full week of useful posts.
